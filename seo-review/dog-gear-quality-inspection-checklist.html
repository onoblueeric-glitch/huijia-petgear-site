Dog Gear Quality Inspection Checklist

Use this dog gear quality inspection checklist to agree what to check before production and shipment. Match each check to the approved sample. Record the result, any open issue and who can approve the order.

By HUIJIA PET·Published September 24, 2026·Updated October 9, 2026/8 min read

01Agree the inspection basis before production

Start with the approved drawing, parts list, size chart, artwork and packed sample. Put the order number and document version on the inspection record. Record any change before checking the goods against it.

This guide is a suggested buying checklist. It does not represent completed testing or a fixed inspection service for every order. Agree the actual scope with the supplier and, where required, an independent inspector or laboratory. Our manufacturing and quality page provides the factory information available on this website.

03Separate visual checks from performance tests

A neat seam and a working buckle are useful checks. They do not prove how much force a finished product can withstand. Agree a separate test with your technical team when a strength claim is needed.

ASTM D6775-13(2024) is a method for determining breaking strength and elongation of textile webbing, tape and braided materials. Its material scope should not be presented as a blanket certification for a finished dog harness, collar or leash.

Name the sample, test method, conditions and pass criteria in the brief. Ask which sizes, colors and parts the report covers. If the webbing, seam or hardware changes, check whether the old result still applies.

Review the evidence behind a product claim

Describe visible features separately from measured results. You can record a front attachment, an adjustable strap or reflective trim in the product specification. A performance or environmental claim needs evidence for that claim.

Evidence to agree before approving claims
Proposed claim or featureRecord to requestApproval boundary
No-pull or training harnessActual leash attachment positions, adjustment layout, sample sizes, intended use and the agreed evaluation method.An attachment layout alone does not prove a training outcome. Keep large and extra-large size evidence tied to the tested construction.
Escape resistance, chew resistance or durabilityIdentified assembly, size and components; fit or performance review conditions, results and limitations relevant to the claim.Do not turn a limited test or material result into an unconditional “escape-proof” or “indestructible” promise.
Reflective collar or harnessReflective material, placement, product size and any agreed visibility assessment and conditions.A reflective feature does not establish a certified visibility level. A result for one component does not cover every finished style.
Recycled content or another environmental claimExact component and claimed proportion, supporting supplier or certification records where applicable, and the product or packaging scope.Durability is separate from environmental evidence. Avoid a broad “eco-friendly” claim based only on appearance or a material name.

For a puppy harness, keep the size chart, adjustment range and fit instructions with the sample. Check each size you plan to sell. A result for a larger adult harness may not apply. Leave unverified claims out of labels and sales copy.

04Define sampling and defect decisions in writing

Before inspection, agree what counts as one lot, how to choose samples and how many to check. If you require an AQL plan, ask the inspector to name the plan and its acceptance rules. A percentage alone is not a complete inspection plan.

List examples of defects and agree how to classify them. Name the person who can hold, rework, replace or release the goods. Keep both the first result and any repeat inspection under the same order and lot number.

05Create a record that supports an actual release decision

  • Order, style, color, size, quantity and specification revision.
  • Sample selection method and the units inspected or tested.
  • Measured values, observations and clearly labeled photographs.
  • Applicable test reports with identifiable samples and scope.
  • Open issues, corrective action and the result of any repeat check.
  • Release decision, date and the authorized approver.

Mark each item as checked, accepted or still open. A filled-in checklist is not permission to ship when failures remain unresolved.

Use the sizing guide to define repeatable dimensional checks and the RFQ workbook to raise inspection requirements before the quotation. For a branded collection, carry the same approved logo and packaging references through private-label development and final inspection.

06What should I approve before bulk dog harness production?

Approve the physical sample and written specification together. Record its style, size, color and version. Include measurements, parts, artwork and pack contents. Name the approver for each item and close open issues before production.

The following is a blank approval framework for a harness program, not a completed test or inspection result.

07Approve a dog leash sample before a bulk order

Use a separate dog leash sample approval record for each construction and option. Write the measured result next to the agreed value. Add a photo reference and mark the decision: accept, revise or open.

Apply the record to the selected HUIJIA reference

For HJ-072, the listed 200 cm length does not define the shortest usable setting. For HJ-007, resolve the measurement condition for the listed 70 or 120 cm option before approving a length. For HJ-056, identify the selected rope diameter and end attachment. None of these reference dimensions establishes a finished-product load rating.
