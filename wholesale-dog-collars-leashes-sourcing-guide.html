How to Source Wholesale Dog Collars and Leashes for a Private-Label Collection

Source wholesale dog collars and leashes by choosing the supply route first: an available resale range or a factory program built to your specification. Then define the collection, quantities, materials, branding, packaging and sample approval before comparing quotations.

01Choose a stock or factory sourcing route

A wholesale order can mean buying an existing brand for resale, ordering an available unbranded style or developing your own collection. Start with the route that matches your launch: products already available for resale, or a specification that needs sampling and production.

Check manufacturing location and shipping location separately. A supplier serving US retailers or shipping from US stock does not by itself establish US manufacture. HUIJIA PET's factory information identifies its manufacturing location in Shangrao, Jiangxi, China.

02Define the collar and leash collection

For one private-label collection, coordinate colors, artwork, labels and hardware across the collar and leash. Approve each item as well as the combined retail pack. A shared print can need different layouts on a narrow collar and a wider leash.

  • Collar: specify construction, minimum and maximum neck adjustment, width, lining and closure.
  • Leash: specify finished length, webbing width or rope diameter, handle and snap hook.
  • Set: identify every included piece, the size pairing, shared packaging and barcode.
  • Assortment: list quantities by style, color and size. Put sets and separately sold pieces on different order lines.

Start with wholesale and custom collars, dog leads and leashes or matching walking sets. For example, the printed collar and leash set provides a two-piece product reference. Confirm the selected variant's sizes, buckle, hook and packing before using it in your range.

03What should a US pet retailer send for a quotation?

Send a single written brief to every shortlisted supplier. Include the selected products, quantities by variant, branding, packaging, delivery destination and requested arrival date. Ask each supplier to identify assumptions and exclusions so the responses can be compared.

Use the RFQ checklist and editable workbook to record the brief and SKU quantities. If a material or hardware choice is open, request separately specified alternatives instead of an unspecified substitute.

04Confirm MOQ, stock and the production schedule

HUIJIA PET programs start from 100 pieces per style, or 100 complete sets for products ordered as sets. Confirm the selected construction, branding, packing and size/color split in the quotation; a listed MOQ does not establish inventory or make every combination eligible.

Ask the supplier to distinguish available stock, sample preparation, production after approval and transport time. For a mixed order, request a schedule for each line and agree whether ready items should wait for custom production. A launch date needs an arrival plan, including the steps after factory dispatch.

Keep the approved sample, artwork version and final specification linked to the quotation. Record changes to the assortment or packaging before confirming the production schedule.

Keep pieces, sets and packing counts separate

Illustrative orderQuotation unitPacking instruction to confirm
100 collars of one style100 pieces, split by the agreed sizes and colors.One collar per selling unit; specify labels and retail packing.

These examples explain counting, not available stock, an accepted assortment or a carton capacity. Ask for carton quantities, dimensions and weights for the approved packed products. For a mixed stock/custom order, record available quantity and dispatch origin on each line, then agree whether to combine or split shipment.

05Approve materials, hardware and the packed sample

Review the material selection with actual swatches and assembled samples. Nylon, polyester and coated-webbing names alone do not specify a finished collar's performance. Match the buckle and adjuster to the strap width, thickness and routing.

  • Measure the collar adjustment limits and leash length using the agreed method.
  • Check buckle engagement, D-ring attachment, hook closure, swivel movement and adjustment grip.
  • Review sewn joints, lining edges, artwork placement, logo attachment and color across the collection.
  • Inspect the selling unit: included pieces, size labels, barcode, instructions and carton marking.
  • Agree performance-test methods and acceptance criteria where needed. Identify the actual sample and test scope in the record.

The quality inspection checklist helps define the approval record. A material or component result should not be presented as proof for every finished collar or leash.

06Compare quotations on the same basis

Compare the same construction, dimensions, quantities, artwork and packaging first. Then separate product cost, samples, setup, retail packing and transport charges. Ask which destination charges are included and which remain for the buyer to arrange. Keep currencies, quotation validity and payment terms visible.

Choose the offer that meets the agreed product and delivery brief. Record the approved specification, unit of sale, quantity split, inspection scope and delivery responsibilities before ordering. This makes a later reorder easier to describe and check.
